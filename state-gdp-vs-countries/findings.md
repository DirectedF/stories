### How to read the ranks

- `rank_among_countries` = 1 + the number of countries with a larger 2025 GDP. States are slotted into the country list one at a time, so states never push each other down, and the United States itself counts as a country (rank 1). Ties would share a rank.
- "Countries" are the 186 World Bank economies (not aggregates) with a 2025 value. The World Bank calls them economies; the list includes some territories, such as Hong Kong SAR (China) and Puerto Rico.
- The `state` type in `combined_ranking.csv` covers the 50 states and the District of Columbia (BEA's state-level areas). Findings about "the 50 states" leave DC out.
- Names: the CSV keeps World Bank names. The charts and this page use short names: Russian Federation → Russia; Korea, Rep. → South Korea; Turkiye → Türkiye; Iran, Islamic Rep. → Iran; Egypt, Arab Rep. → Egypt; Hong Kong SAR, China → Hong Kong SAR (China); Macao SAR, China → Macao SAR (China); Viet Nam → Vietnam; Slovak Republic → Slovakia.

## Limits

- **Exchange rates, not purchasing power.** Country GDP is converted to dollars at market exchange rates, so a currency swing moves a country up or down with no change in what it produces. Measured at purchasing power parity, many lower-income countries would look larger. States are already in dollars.
- **Estimates get revised, and some are missing.** BEA revised its 2025 state figures on September 30, 2026 and will revise them again; World Bank 2025 values are first estimates and will also change. 31 economies have no 2025 value and are left out, including the United Arab Emirates (see check e for what that changes).
- **States are not separate economies.** They share one currency, one central bank and one federal budget, and trade freely with each other. The comparison is about size, not about what a state could do on its own.
- **Two publishers, two vintages.** State values are BEA's latest; country values, including the United States, are the World Bank's. The two U.S. totals differ slightly (check b). State GDP also leaves out U.S. activity abroad (check a).
